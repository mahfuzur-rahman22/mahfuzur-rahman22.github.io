What I Work On

Research Interests

The problems that pull me are ones where material behaviour, structural mechanics, and computational modelling intersect, particularly in aerospace contexts where getting it wrong is not an option.

🧱
Composite & Multi-Material Structures
How fibre-reinforced and metal matrix composites carry load, accumulate damage, and fail, especially under combined loading conditions representative of real aerospace service environments.
🌡️
Thermo-Mechanical Coupling
The interaction between thermal gradients and mechanical stress fields in anisotropic materials, a critical design challenge for structures in high-temperature aerospace and propulsion applications.
💻
Computational Structural Mechanics
Building finite difference and finite element frameworks, writing numerical solvers in MATLAB and Python, validating against FEA tools such as ANSYS and ABAQUS — to study problems that resist closed-form solutions.
✈️
Aerospace Structural Design
Applying structural and aerodynamic analysis to real airframe design, balancing weight, stiffness, and strength in competition and research-grade aircraft from concept through physical build.
🚁
Fixed-Wing UAV Design
Aerodynamic design, structural sizing, and manufacturing of fixed-wing UAVs, integrating XFLR5, AVL, and composite fabrication techniques in competition and research contexts.
🔬
CFD & Numerical Flow Modelling
Computational fluid dynamics and numerical modelling of aerospace flow fields, understanding how airflows interact with structural surfaces under varying flight conditions.

Thermomechanical Behaviour of Anisotropic Metal Matrix Composite Panels
Undergraduate Thesis  ·  Supervisor: Prof. Dr. Shaikh Reaz Ahmed, Dept. of Mechanical Engineering, BUET
Problem How do thermal stress fields develop and concentrate in anisotropic Metal Matrix Composite (MMC) panels under combined thermo-mechanical loading — and where are failure-prone zones most likely to form?
Method Built a displacement-function-based finite-difference computational framework in MATLAB to solve the coupled thermoelastic field (stress, displacement, temperature) across orthotropic composite panel geometries under prescribed boundary conditions.
Outcome Solver accuracy exceeds ABAQUS FEM benchmarks under equivalent boundary conditions. Characterised stress concentration zones as functions of fibre volume fraction and temperature level — validated against ANSYS thermal–structural simulations.
MATLAB Finite Difference Method Metal Matrix Composites Thermal Stress Analysis ANSYS Validation Anisotropic Materials Displacement-Function Approach
